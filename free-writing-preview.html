The first frost arrived in the mountain village before the sun did. It silvered the empty market stalls and held the last fallen leaves against the steps of the bakery. From her window, Mira watched the path disappear into a pale morning.

She made lanterns for a living, although she had never learned to feel at home in the dark. Each evening she counted the candles before locking her workshop. Tonight, there was only one left.

A knock came just as she was closing the shutters. Her elderly neighbour stood outside with a brass lantern and a request: would she take it to the old watchtower? His knees could no longer manage the climb. Someone, he said, might still be looking for its light.

Mira wanted to ask who. Instead, she tucked the lantern under her coat and stepped onto the path. The flame trembled each time the wind found a seam in the brass. When the last cottage disappeared behind her, she stopped. Then she took one more step.

From the watchtower steps, her lantern caught a small red mitten between the trees. A child stood beneath it, too frightened to call out. Mira lowered the light until the boy could see her face. Together they walked back toward the village, following the footprints she had nearly turned away from.

By morning, nothing in the village had changed very much. But Mira left a lantern in her window, and when the shadows gathered that evening, she let them come a little closer.
